How Many Deals Does a Game of Hazari to 1,000 Take?

I simulated 400 whole Hazari games to find out. The average is about eight deals, the shortest possible is three, and the results show why skill shortens games.

Tags

  • Hazari
  • Card Games
  • Simulation
  • Game Length

Published November 24, 2026.

You are about to start a game of Hazari to 1,000. How long will it last? Players guess anything from a handful of deals to an evening that never ends. I simulated 400 complete games to get a number.

How I tested it

Four computer players played each game under the standard rules: 13 cards each, groups of 3, 3, 3 and 4, scores carried from deal to deal, and the game ending when someone reaches 1,000. In this first test every player arranged their cards at random, within the rules. That makes it a floor for skill, not a picture of how people play.

The simulation also assumes the deal passes to the next player each time and the player after the dealer leads the first round. If your table differs, the exact numbers will shift a little.

What came out

  • Average: 7.9 deals per game.
  • Middle game: 8 deals.
  • Shortest: 3 deals. Longest: 11 deals.
  • Typical range: 8 games in 10 lasted between 6 and 10 deals.

The minimum of three deals is not luck. One deal is worth 360 points, so two deals can give a player at most 720. Reaching 1,000 needs at least three, and that needs a near-perfect run.

Does the seat matter?

Across the 400 games the four seats won 95, 92, 107 and 106 times. That is close to an even split of 100 each, so I found no sign that sitting first or last helps. Two games ended with two players tied for the top score.

Skill shortens the game

Strong arrangement ends games faster, because one player keeps winning rounds and the points pile up in one place.

  • One player choosing the best of 40 random arrangements against three random players won all 200 simulated games, in about 4.3 deals on average.
  • One player using an exhaustive search for the best arrangement, against the same three random players, won all 60 simulated games, in about 3.3 deals on average.

So if a game drags on, a likely reason is that nobody is arranging much better than anyone else. A table of evenly matched players trades points around for longer.

Planning an evening

Plan for around eight deals, and be ready for six or ten. Time one deal at your table and multiply. If you want a shorter game, a lower target is easy to agree on; the house rules checklist covers it.
