Paper or App? How to Keep Hazari Score Without Arguments

An honest comparison of keeping Hazari score on paper, in a notes app and in a scorekeeper, with a simple paper layout that makes errors visible.

Tags

  • Hazari
  • Card Games
  • Scoring
  • Scorekeeper

Published November 24, 2026.

Paper has run Hazari games for generations. A phone is in everyone's pocket now. Which keeps the better score? It depends on what you value, and both can go wrong in predictable ways.

Paper

  • Strengths: free, needs no battery, everyone can see it at once and nothing can freeze.
  • Weaknesses: crossing out is messy, and running totals have to be added by hand. A single slip carries into every later row.

A phone notes app

  • Strengths: easy to share, easy to edit.
  • Weaknesses: you have to type the whole running total every time, and it is easy to overwrite the wrong number.

A scorekeeper app

  • Strengths: adds the totals itself, ranks the players, checks that a round adds up, keeps every round so a mistake can be found, and can share a live view.
  • Weaknesses: it needs a phone, and one person must be trusted to enter the scores.

A paper layout that makes mistakes visible

If you stay with paper, use this layout:

  1. One column per player and one row per deal.
  2. A last column on the right for the row check, where you add the four points and write 360.
  3. A separate block for running totals, so you never overwrite a points row.
  4. A single line under the totals showing 360 times the number of deals, to compare with the total of the four running scores.

Those two checks, the row sum and the grand total, catch nearly every addition error. The article on settling scoring arguments shows how to use them.

When an app is worth it

A scorekeeper helps most when you play long games, when the table argues, or when people who are not at the table want to follow along. If you only play a short game with four people sitting around one sheet of paper, paper is fine.
