One Hazari Deal, Round by Round: A Worked Example with Real Cards

A full four-player Hazari deal with every hand, every group and every round shown, so you can see how groups are compared and how 360 points get split.

The easiest way to learn Hazari is to watch a deal played through. I generated this one with the game engine, using four medium-strength computer players, and checked that the points add up to 360.

The four arrangements

Each player's 13 cards, split into groups of 3, 3, 3 and 4, strongest first:

  • Player 1: J♦ 10♣ 9♦ (Run) · 10♠ 6♠ 2♠ (Colour) · K♥ K♦ 8♦ (Pair) · Q♠ Q♣ J♣ 4♥ (Pair of Queens)
  • Player 2: A♦ Q♦ 5♦ (Colour) · K♣ 7♦ 7♣ (Pair) · 9♠ 4♠ 4♣ (Pair) · 10♥ 5♣ 3♠ 3♥ (Pair of 3s)
  • Player 3: K♠ 8♠ 7♠ (Colour) · 8♣ 6♣ 2♣ (Colour) · 7♥ 6♥ 2♥ (Colour) · A♠ J♠ J♥ 9♣ (Pair of Jacks)
  • Player 4: A♥ 8♥ 5♥ (Colour) · 10♦ 6♦ 4♦ (Colour) · 9♥ 3♦ 3♣ (Pair) · A♣ Q♥ 5♠ 2♦ (Indi)

Check one: Player 4's last group is A♣ Q♥ 5♠ 2♦. Its best three cards are A, Q and 5, an Indi, and it is the weakest group in that hand, so the arrangement is legal. Play goes in order from Player 2, who leads the first round in this simulation.

Round 1: a Run beats three Colours

Players compare their first groups. Players 2, 3 and 4 all show a Colour, but Player 1 shows a Run (J-10-9), and a Run beats a Colour. Player 1 wins all 12 cards, worth 90 points.

Round 2: the higher Colour wins

Three players show a Colour. Player 4's 10-6-4 of diamonds beats Player 1's 10-6-2 of spades, because the first two cards tie and the 4 is higher than the 2. Player 2's Pair loses to any Colour. Player 4 wins 75 points.

Round 3: one Colour among Pairs

Player 3's Colour (7-6-2 of hearts) beats three Pairs, the Kings of Player 1, the 4s of Player 2 and the 3s of Player 4. Player 3 wins 70 points.

Round 4: the big pile

Everyone shows four cards, and the best three decide. Player 1 has a Pair of Queens, Player 3 a Pair of Jacks, Player 2 a Pair of 3s, and Player 4 has no Pair. Queens win. This round holds 16 cards, so Player 1 collects 125 points.

The result

  • Player 1: 90 + 125 = 215
  • Player 4: 75
  • Player 3: 70
  • Player 2: 0

215 + 75 + 70 + 0 = 360, as it must. Player 2 had a respectable hand and still won nothing: a Pair or a Colour was always beaten by something stronger in the same round.

What to take from it

  • Rounds are won by comparing groups in the same position, not by comparing whole hands.
  • A single Run or Colour in the right position can win a whole round.
  • The four-card group decided the biggest pile, as the article on the four-card group describes.
